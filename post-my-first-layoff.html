My first tech layoff

A locked laptop, a group chat on fire, and the three months that followed.

Alejandro Sandoval Nov 14, 2026 · 4 min read
  • career
  • layoff
  • support engineering

This is the story of my first layoff. I'm telling it as it happened to me, not as an analysis of the industry.

May 7, 2026

I was two months into my job as a technical support engineer at Cloudflare. It was a normal workday. Around 1:30 pm I took a break to go eat, and when I came back my laptop was locked: a black screen with a banner that said to contact my IT administrator.

It looked like ransomware, but I knew it was something else. I opened WhatsApp and the Cloudflare Mexico group and my team's group were blowing up. I didn't understand what was happening, so I sent my manager a DM. Instead of replying, they called me, gave me the context, and asked whether I had been affected.

That's when it clicked. I opened my personal email and there it was: the email I least expected to get.

Distrust, anger and helplessness.

The offer I had said no to

About two weeks earlier I had turned down an offer from a Microsoft recruiter for a Cloud Solutions Architect role. After the layoff I felt like an idiot for saying no. And when I went looking, that opening was gone.

Applying, slowly

I took a few days to process what had happened. Then I started applying, but not in bulk. I was very selective: I applied to maybe five roles, one of them a technical support engineer position at Microsoft.

I got the screening and then the interviews. I prepared, but not intensely. I already had experience with these processes and I knew what a technical round for support looks like.

Where I am now

It's funny how, sometimes, effort, hard work and a bit of luck can make things better much faster than you'd think. Today I'm happy and motivated by my own expectations and goals. I'm somewhere I can learn from a lot of talented people, my team has been very supportive, and my best friend also joined as a technical support engineer.

If you're going through this right now

Don't get discouraged. The industry is going through a "restructuring" (a euphemism) and it's a complicated moment. Never stop learning, today more than ever. Experiences like this give you a chance to reinvent yourself.

I wish we all reinvented ourselves out of vocation and not out of necessity. What matters is to see where you come from, where you are, and where you're going, because AI will keep amplifying the changes in tech roles.

If you made it this far, thank you for reading. If you ever need advice, or think I could help with something, write me on X or LinkedIn.

Alejandro Sandoval
Technical Support Engineer, writing it down in public from Mexico City.
